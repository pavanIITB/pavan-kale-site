When a habit competes with work, errands, or a phone full of alerts, an extra reminder can become another thing to ignore. It helps to think about where the action already happens.

Put the cue beside the thing someone needs to use. Then see whether they notice it and can act without stopping to figure anything out. For a water bottle, the next step should be obvious the moment the reminder appears and the bottle is in reach.

The timing needs its own test. A cue that is welcome while someone is at a desk can be annoying during a meeting, and an alert that repeats too often can disappear into the background. Watch how people respond across several days. If they start switching the cue off, find out when they decided to do that and what they were doing at the time.

## Pay attention to the second week

The first day tells you whether someone can understand the cue. The second week tells you more about whether the cue belongs in the routine at all. You might see people respond while the product is new, then start ignoring it once it becomes a familiar part of the desk. Ask whether they noticed the reminder on an ordinary afternoon and what they were doing when it appeared.

A useful follow-up might be a short note after a few days, asking about the last time they took a sip after seeing the cue. That gives you an example you can compare with other responses. It can also reveal a quieter success, like the device prompting someone to act without interrupting the work they cared about.
