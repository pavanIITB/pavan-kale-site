When you’ve spent too long on a problem, write down what you know, what you’ve already tried, and the next thing you’d test. Then take a walk or do something that uses a different kind of attention.

The note gives you a specific place to start when you return. Sometimes the next test is still the right one. Sometimes the act of writing it down makes it obvious that you’ve been taking something for granted.

Choose a stopping point you can recognize. If you have tried changing the same thing three times without learning anything new, parking it for an hour may be more useful than a fourth attempt. Put enough detail in the note that it could help a teammate as well. Coming back with a fresh idea is useful. Coming back with a specific place to start is already progress.

## Make the break easy to return from

A break is more useful when you leave a small trail. Keep the files or tools you were using where they can be found. Record the last result and the test you thought was worth running next. That reduces the effort of remembering the whole situation when you sit down again.

It’s fine if returning doesn’t produce a sudden breakthrough. You can try the next test with a little more patience, or decide that you need a second person to look at the problem. The short note is useful in either case. It gives you something specific to carry into a conversation and makes it easier for somebody else to make a suggestion.
