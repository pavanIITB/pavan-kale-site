Suppose a customer says a reminder is easy to miss. Ask when it happened and where the product was. A reminder in a bag, across the room, or beside your keyboard is really three different cases. Look for the conditions that recur in a few comments.

Turn that pattern into a question. Can someone working at a desk see the current cue without deliberately looking for it? Run the current version first. Then change one variable, such as the angle of the light or when it appears. Ask the same thing again and compare what people actually do.

Tell the customer which situation you tested if you go back to them. They may point out a detail that changes the interpretation. The best result of the conversation is often a clearer idea of what happens in real use and a test you can run on the next version.

## Keep the result close to the original comment

Save the customer’s example with the notes from the test. The example tells you what you were trying to understand. The test tells you how far you got. If a cue became easier to see at a desk, record the conditions of that test before treating the result as general.

That record makes it easier to decide what to investigate next. You may want to see if the change holds up outdoors or if it affects battery life. You may find that only the original setup needed a change. Either result is useful when the next person can trace it back to the situation that prompted the work.
