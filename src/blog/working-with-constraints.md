---
title: Making trade-offs visible
description: A way to decide what to protect when time and resources are limited.
date: 2026-11-26
sample: true
---

It helps to separate a hard constraint from a preference. A part that arrives after the next test is a hard constraint for that test. Wanting the enclosure to look finished is usually a preference at the prototype stage. Put both on paper so the team can talk about the difference.

Give the things you could change a name. You might keep the deadline and narrow the test to one behavior, or keep the scope and wait for the component you really need. State what you’ll learn in either case. A smaller test can be a good decision if it answers the question that would make the next week of work worthwhile.

Write down the cost of the choice as well. If you use a temporary part, record which results may change when you switch back. That makes the trade-off easier to revisit once the original constraint goes away.
